# Deponent Verbs

---

## 1. What a Deponent Verb Is

A **deponent** verb has **no active forms**: it conjugates entirely with middle (or middle/passive) endings, yet its meaning is **active**.

| Verb | Form | Meaning |
|------|------|---------|
| λύομαι (from λύω) | middle/passive of an active verb | "I am loosed" / "I loose for myself" |
| **ἔρχομαι** | deponent — no active exists | simply "I come" |

Deponency is a property of the **word**, not of a tense or an ending. There is no special "deponent conjugation" to learn: deponents reuse exactly the middle/passive paradigms you meet in the ordinary voice lessons of this book. What this lesson teaches is how to **recognize** these verbs and how to **translate** them.

**The dictionary is the test:** if the headword ends in **‑μαι** (βούλομαι, γίγνομαι, δύναμαι), the verb is deponent. If the headword ends in ‑ω or ‑μι (λύω, δίδωμι), a ‑μαι form you meet in a text is a true middle or passive.

## 2. Common Deponents Worth Knowing

| Verb | Meaning | Note |
|------|------|------|
| **ἔρχομαι** | to come, go | suppletive active aorist ἦλθον (§3.4) |
| **γίγνομαι** | to become, happen | aorist ἐγενόμην; perfect γέγονα is active in form |
| **βούλομαι** | to want, wish | passive deponent: aorist ἐβουλήθην (§3.2) |
| **δύναμαι** | to be able | athematic (§3.3); aorist ἐδυνήθην |
| **δέχομαι** | to receive, accept | middle deponent: aorist ἐδεξάμην |
| **μάχομαι** | to fight | takes the dative; aorist ἐμαχεσάμην |
| **ἀποκρίνομαι** | to answer | takes the dative |
| **αἰσθάνομαι** | to perceive | takes the genitive; aorist ᾐσθόμην |
| **ἡγέομαι** | to lead; to consider | ε‑contract, conjugates like the middle of ποιέω |
| **πορεύομαι** | to travel, march | passive deponent: aorist ἐπορεύθην |
| **φοβέομαι** | to fear | ε‑contract passive deponent: aorist ἐφοβήθην |
| **ἐπίσταμαι** | to know, understand | athematic (§3.3) |

## 3. The Four Situations to Recognize

### 3.1 Middle deponents
Their aorist is **middle** in form: ἐδεξάμην "I received," ἐμαχεσάμην "I fought," ἐγενόμην "I became." Conjugation: exactly the aorist middle paradigms of this book.

### 3.2 Passive deponents
Their aorist is **passive** in form (the θη‑aorist), still with active meaning: ἐβουλήθην "I wanted," ἐφοβήθην "I feared," ἐπορεύθην "I traveled." When you meet a θη‑form of one of these verbs in the aorist‑passive lessons, translate it actively.

### 3.3 Athematic deponents
δύναμαι, ἐπίσταμαι, κεῖμαι, κάθημαι attach the middle endings **directly to the stem**, without a thematic vowel: δύνα‑μαι, δύνα‑σαι, δύνα‑ται, δυνά‑μεθα, δύνα‑σθε, δύνα‑νται. They belong with the **‑μι verbs**, and this book's (mi) lessons conjugate them.

### 3.4 Suppletive active tenses
A few deponents borrow **active‑form** tenses from other stems: ἔρχομαι has aorist **ἦλθον** and perfect **ἐλήλυθα**; γίγνομαι has perfect **γέγονα**. These forms conjugate like ordinary active aorists and perfects — only the dictionary form tells you they belong to a deponent verb.

## 4. Key Rules & Common Pitfalls

| Rule | Explanation | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **1. Translate actively** | The form is middle/passive; the meaning is not. | βούλεται "he wants," not "he is wanted" |
| **2. The headword decides** | Only a ‑μαι dictionary form marks a deponent. | λύομαι is *not* deponent (headword λύω) |
| **3. Learn the principal parts** | The aorist may be middle (ἐγενόμην), passive (ἐβουλήθην), or active suppletive (ἦλθον). | see §3 |
| **4. Don't confuse with meaningful middles** | Some active verbs have a middle with its own meaning — that is not deponency. | ἄρχω "I rule" / ἄρχομαι "I begin"; πείθω "I persuade" / πείθομαι "I obey" |
| **5. Middle futures of active verbs** | Several common active verbs have a future that is middle in form only ("semi‑deponents"). | ἀκούω → ἀκούσομαι, λαμβάνω → λήψομαι, ὁράω → ὄψομαι, βαίνω → βήσομαι |
| **6. Agent phrases are absent** | A deponent is not a true passive, so no ὑπό + genitive agent accompanies it. | γίγνεται ταῦτα "these things happen" |

## 5. Example Sentences

| Greek | Transliteration | Translation |
|----------------------------|----------------|-------------|
| **ἔρχομαι εἰς τὴν πόλιν.** | érkhomai eis tḕn pólin | I am coming into the city. |
| **οἱ στρατιῶται μάχονται τοῖς πολεμίοις.** | hoi stratiôtai mákhontai toîs polemíois | The soldiers fight the enemy. |
| **ἐβουλήθη ὁ βασιλεὺς ἀκοῦσαι.** | eboulḗthē ho basileùs akoûsai | The king wanted to listen. (passive form, active meaning) |
| **οὐ δύναμαι λέγειν.** | ou dýnamai légein | I am not able to speak. (athematic) |
| **ἦλθον οἱ ξένοι εἰς τὴν οἰκίαν.** | êlthon hoi xénoi eis tḕn oikían | The strangers came into the house. (suppletive active aorist of ἔρχομαι) |
| **ὁ διδάσκαλος φιλεῖται ὑπὸ τῶν μαθητῶν.** | ho didáskalos phileîtai hypò tôn mathētôn | The teacher is loved by the students. (true passive, for contrast) |

## 6. Usage & Context

1. **Deponents are everywhere.** Verbs of motion, feeling, perception, and speech are disproportionately deponent (ἔρχομαι, βούλομαι, αἰσθάνομαι, ἀποκρίνομαι); in most Greek prose a large share of the middle‑looking forms you meet are deponents. Trust the meaning, not the shape.
2. **In this book** deponent forms appear inside the regular voice lessons, since their endings are the regular middle/passive endings; the answer keys flag each one as *deponent — active in meaning* so you are never left guessing.
3. **The term** comes from Latin *dēpōnere* "to lay aside": these verbs have, as it were, laid their active forms aside. Modern grammars often prefer "middle‑only verbs" (*media tantum*), stressing that for many of them the middle voice is meaningful — verbs of personal involvement — rather than an arbitrary gap.

## 7. Quick Reference

| Situation | Recognize by | Example | Translate |
|-----------|--------------|---------|-----------|
| Middle deponent | headword ‑μαι, middle aorist | δέχομαι, ἐδεξάμην | actively |
| Passive deponent | headword ‑μαι, θη‑aorist | βούλομαι, ἐβουλήθην | actively |
| Athematic deponent | headword ‑μαι, no thematic vowel | δύναμαι, δύνασαι | actively |
| Suppletive active tense | active form, ‑μαι headword | ἦλθον ← ἔρχομαι | actively |
| Meaningful middle (not deponent) | headword ‑ω exists | ἄρχομαι ← ἄρχω | by its middle sense |
| Semi‑deponent future | active verb, middle future | ἀκούσομαι ← ἀκούω | actively |
